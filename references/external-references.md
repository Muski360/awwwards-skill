# External Research

Use before choosing a new visual direction or substantial visual redesign, and when an unfamiliar mechanism needs investigation. Follow the exceptions in [SKILL.md](../SKILL.md). Research should change a decision or resolve uncertainty; reading this guide or its catalogue does not satisfy that work.

## Research From a Project Question

1. **Frame the opportunity.** Start with the audience, content, and available material. Ask what the experience could reveal, organize, evoke, or let someone do. Inspect relevant supplied references before substituting familiar favorites. Search for the question rather than a predetermined effect or style.
2. **Discover beyond the catalogue.** Use [Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/websites/) for executed experiences, [Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/) for experiments and techniques, and open-source projects for inspectable implementations. Follow useful entries to the actual site, creator, demo, or repository. Also investigate sources specific to the subject: archives, objects, documents, maps, publications, photography, or other relevant media. Seek contrasting structures and quieter work as well as spectacle. These are discovery routes, not a mandatory tour or a closed shortlist. The [reference catalogue](reference-catalog.md) is a conditional mechanism index, not the starting aesthetic.
3. **Inspect the evidence.** Open selected sources and examine the relevant composition or behavior using [visual reference analysis](visual-identity.md#read-screenshots-recordings-and-references). Identify the relationship worth learning and why it works in that context. Label evidence as live observation, image/recording, author documentation, source code, or inference. Search snippets establish leads, not visual findings; code does not prove runtime behavior. If a source is blocked, use another accessible route or source instead of repeated blind retries, and disclose what remains unobserved.
4. **Adapt and decide.** Contrast approaches through the project's own material. Keep a brief note only for sources that affect the choice: URL and evidence location, transferable principle, project-specific adaptation, and consequential unknown. A palette swap or collage of recognizable fragments is insufficient adaptation. Stop when the evidence supports a direction or a focused prototype; broaden the search if every candidate repeats the initial structure. Do not collect links or effects to meet a quota.
5. **Trace implementation when needed.** Before reusing code or selecting a dependency, inspect its relevant entry point, manifest, current authoritative documentation, and license. Distinguish the mechanism from demo scaffolding. Verify stack/router fit, browser and SSR assumptions, lifecycle, input alternatives, and failure behavior through [motion and implementation](motion-and-implementation.md). Check media and font rights separately. Public code and a successful demo do not establish production readiness or permission to reuse every asset.

## Carry Research Into the Work

Prototype the uncertainty that could change the direction, with real content and assets. Judge it in the surrounding experience: does it improve identity, hierarchy, rhythm, feedback, understanding, or atmosphere? Adapt or discard a mechanism when the reference dominates the project or its cost outweighs its contribution.

Cite influential sources in working notes or the handoff, not as decorative interface labels. Report what was actually opened, observed, and tested. When tools or sources are unavailable, distinguish a provisional direction from one supported by inspected visual evidence; never invent references or measurements.
