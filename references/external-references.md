# External References and Technical Research

Use when research could improve a visual direction, reveal a stronger interaction, or resolve implementation uncertainty. Start from the project's context and material. References expand the available vocabulary of composition, mechanisms, motion, and implementation; they do not determine the result or create a requirement to add effects.

## Research From a Project Question

1. **Define what to learn.** Identify an opportunity in the content or experience: framing a body of work, pacing a long narrative, maintaining continuity between routes, or making an interaction feel connected to its subject. Keep the question open to different visual and technical answers. Inspect relevant supplied references before replacing them with familiar favorites.
2. **Search beyond the starting points when useful.** Seek additional high-quality references before choosing a solution if the project would benefit from a broader repertoire. Favor established experiments, maintained libraries, award-winning sites, Codrops-style demos, and well-executed open-source interactions. Search by the design question, medium, or mechanism; include platform constraints when researching feasibility. Follow galleries and awards to the actual site, creator, demo, or source. Prestige and popularity help discovery but do not establish project fit, accessibility, or performance. Include composition, typography, media framing, and quieter work; do not let an effects-heavy shortlist define the direction.
3. **Observe, then trace.** Use the [visual reference analysis](visual-identity.md#read-screenshots-recordings-and-references) to inspect the relevant sequence and its surrounding composition. Look at trigger, progression, overlap, rest, reversal, and what remains stable. A recording shows behavior; the author's source and documentation explain an implementation. Neither proves the other matches the current release. Distinguish what you observed, what the author documents, and what you infer. If a live demo is unavailable, continue from accessible evidence and name the gap.
4. **Compare and stop with a decision.** Compare materially different approaches where the choice is uncertain, including a static composition or an existing mechanism. Stop when you can explain the project fit, adaptation, likely implementation, and unresolved risk well enough to choose or prototype. Do not browse the entire catalogue, meet a reference quota, or collect effects without a design question. A narrow, understood fix does not require a research detour.

## Translate the Idea Into Project Work

Keep a brief decision note for references that affect the direction: source and evidence location; the transferable relationship; why it fits this project; what changes in composition, subject, type, timing, or input; and the implementation choice with remaining unknowns. A palette swap or combining recognizable fragments from several sites is not sufficient adaptation. Build a shared visual and motion language from the project's identity, rather than preserving each demo's separate style.

Before reusing code or choosing a dependency, inspect the relevant entry point, package manifest, current authoritative documentation, and license. Distinguish library requirements from demo scaffolding. Check compatibility with the installed stack, browser/SSR assumptions, lifecycle cleanup, and required input/fallback behavior using [motion and implementation](motion-and-implementation.md). Check media/font rights separately from code licensing. A public repository or successful demo does not establish permission to reuse every asset or readiness for the target project. Avoid importing an entire starter, copying old setup commands, or relying on private APIs without a verified reason.

Prototype the uncertain part with the project's real content and assets. Judge the rendered result in its surrounding page and relevant input conditions: did the idea improve identity, hierarchy, rhythm, feedback, or atmosphere, and does it remain coherent beyond the showpiece? Adapt further or discard it when the reference dominates the project or its cost outweighs its contribution. Cite influential sources in working notes or the handoff, not as decorative interface labels; report checks actually performed and evidence still missing.
